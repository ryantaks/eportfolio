Project 1: Beyond Irresponsibility — Understanding the Complexity of College Student Financial Behavior

February 13, 2026

This paper started with a question I actually found interesting: why do college students keep making bad financial decisions even when they know better? The research made it clear pretty quickly that the answer isn't laziness — it's a lot more complicated than that. Going through peer-reviewed studies for the first time was a different experience than I expected. The sources didn't always agree with each other, and learning how to work with that tension instead of just ignoring it was probably the most useful thing I got out of this project. The annotated bibliography pushed me to actually understand each source rather than just summarize it, which made writing the paper itself a lot more straightforward.

The common perception of college-age individuals is to view them as bad with money. However, when we examine this issue through peer-reviewed research, it loses a lot of its appeal. College student financial troubles occur for many reasons other than laziness or immaturity; there are objective gaps in the student's financial literacy, psychological influences on the student's behaviour, and differences between objective and subjective knowledge about finances.

Reflection

Reflecting on ENC 2135

As my time in Research, Genre, and Context comes to an end, I can honestly say my writing improved more than I expected. Going into this class, I thought I already had a pretty good understanding of writing from my previous courses. I figured it would mostly be the same things I've already learned — basic structure, grammar, and just making sure everything flows. But this class showed me that writing is actually a lot deeper than that. I learned that genre isn't just about what type of writing something is, but more about the purpose behind it and how it connects to the audience. The rhetorical situation plays a big role in everything, including tone, structure, and even small details that I never really paid attention to before. Things like visuals, layout, and even word choice can completely change how a message comes across. Before this class, I didn't really think about that kind of stuff, but now I see that writing is not just about what you say, it's about how you say it and who you're saying it to.

Project 1

While working on Project 1, my research skills definitely improved. At the beginning, I wasn't really sure how to find strong sources, and I would just pick things that seemed relevant without thinking too much about how reliable they actually were. Over time, I got better at using the Florida State University databases and finding more credible, scholarly sources. I also learned how to actually use those sources instead of just summarizing them. Before, I would kind of just include information and move on, but this project forced me to explain how each source connects to my argument and why it matters. The annotated bibliography helped a lot with this because it made me slow down and actually think about each source instead of just throwing it in to meet a requirement. It also helped me understand how different sources can work together to support a bigger idea. Overall, this project showed me that research isn't just about finding information, it's about using it in a way that actually strengthens your argument.

Project 2

Project 2 was more focused on rhetorical analysis, and even though I had some experience with that, this class pushed me to go further with it. In the past, I mostly focused on what the author was saying, but not really how they were saying it. This project made me look at things like visuals, tone, and credibility in a more detailed way. I started to realize that different genres communicate the same idea in completely different ways depending on the audience. For example, something on social media is going to be a lot more direct and simple compared to a scholarly article, even if they're talking about the same topic. This project also made me more aware of how persuasion actually works. I started noticing that authors make very intentional choices to influence their audience, whether it's through emotional appeal, credibility, or the way information is presented. After this, I definitely started looking at everyday media differently because I can now see the strategy behind it instead of just reading it at face value.

Project 3

Project 3 was probably my favorite because it was more creative compared to the other projects. Instead of just analyzing or researching, I had to create my own pieces using different genres. At first, I didn't think it would be that difficult, but it actually made me realize how hard it is to effectively communicate a message to different audiences. Each genre required a different approach, and I had to really think about how I wanted my message to come across. This project also made me more aware of my own writing strengths and weaknesses. Some parts came easier to me, while others took more effort and time to get right. Reflecting on my own work helped me understand what I did well and what I could improve on. It also showed me that writing is more of a process than I thought, and that it takes time to develop something that actually works.

Revision

Another important thing I learned in this class was how to properly revise my writing. Before, I would usually just fix grammar mistakes or reword a few sentences and consider it done. But this class taught me that revision is way more than that. Global revision means looking at the bigger picture — like your thesis, organization, and whether your ideas actually make sense. When I revised my project, I had to go back and rethink how everything was structured instead of just making small edits. This was honestly more difficult, but it made a big difference in the final version. I realized that sometimes you have to make bigger changes to actually improve your writing instead of just fixing small mistakes. This is something I'll definitely keep in mind moving forward because it made my work a lot stronger overall.

Overall

Overall, this course helped me grow as a writer in ways I didn't expect. I learned how to better understand my audience, use stronger research, and think more critically about my writing. I also gained a better understanding of how important revision is and how much it can improve a piece when done correctly. These are all things I know I'll use in future classes and even outside of school. Writing is something I now see as more of a process instead of just something you finish and turn in. I feel more confident in my ability to communicate my ideas clearly, and I know I have a better foundation to build on going forward.
